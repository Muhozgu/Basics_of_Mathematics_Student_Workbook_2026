# Solution 10

For products, determinants multiply:

$$
\det(AB)=\det(A)\det(B)=2\cdot5=\boxed{10}.
$$

Transposition does not change a determinant, and multiplying a $3\times3$
matrix by the scalar $2$ multiplies its determinant by $2^3$:

$$
\det(2A^T)=2^3\det(A^T)=8\det(A)=8\cdot2=\boxed{16}.
$$

Swapping two rows changes the sign of the determinant. Multiplying one row
by $3$ then multiplies the determinant by $3$. Thus

$$
\det(C)=3\bigl(-\det(A)\bigr)=-3\cdot2=\boxed{-6}.
$$
