# Solution 9

Solve the system

$$
\begin{cases}x+y=3\\2x-y=0\\3x=3\end{cases}
$$

From the third equation,

$$
3x=3\quad\Rightarrow\quad x=1.
$$

Substitute into $x+y=3$:

$$
1+y=3\quad\Rightarrow\quad y=2.
$$

Check the second equation:

$$
2(1)-2=0,
$$

which is true. So the solution is

$$
\boxed{(x,y)=(1,2)}.
$$

Three equations in two unknowns need not be inconsistent because the extra equations may be redundant or consistent with one another. Here, the third equation is compatible with the first two, so the system has a valid common solution.
