# Exercise 1 Solution

## Problem
Compute
$$
\det\begin{pmatrix}3&-2\\5&4\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\det\begin{pmatrix}1&7\\2&14\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Which matrix is invertible? Justify your answer.

## Solution
For a $2\times2$ matrix $\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}$,
$$
\det = ad-bc.
$$

So,
$$
\det\begin{pmatrix}3&-2\\5&4\end{pmatrix}=3\cdot4-(-2)\cdot5=12+10=22,
$$

and
$$
\det\begin{pmatrix}1&7\\2&14\end{pmatrix}=1\cdot14-7\cdot2=14-14=0.
$$

Therefore, the first matrix is invertible because its determinant is nonzero, and the second matrix is not invertible because its determinant is zero.

## Final Answer
$$
\det\begin{pmatrix}3&-2\\5&4\end{pmatrix}=22,\qquad
\det\begin{pmatrix}1&7\\2&14\end{pmatrix}=0.
$$
The first matrix is invertible; the second is not.
